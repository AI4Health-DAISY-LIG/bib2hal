Why this tool?

HAL is the national open archive used by French research institutions. Your HAL record is what your laboratory, your university and evaluation committees look at — but most of us keep our list up to date somewhere else.

1

HAL is where you are counted

Laboratory reports, institutional open-science indicators and individual evaluation files increasingly rely on HAL. A publication that is not in HAL, or not linked to your IdHAL, is easily missed.

2

Depositing one by one is slow

HAL's web form handles one paper at a time. With tens of papers to catch up, the backlog never gets done. bib2hal handles the whole list in one pass and only asks you about what needs a decision.

3

No duplicates, no surprises

Before anything is sent, bib2hal looks every reference up in HAL, spots duplicates and possible homonyms, completes the metadata from Crossref, and lets HAL validate each record without creating it.

What it does

  • Imports a BibTeX file exported from Google Scholar, Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote or ORCID — how to get one.
  • Completes each reference from Crossref (DOI, authors, journal, conference place and date).
  • Checks HAL: already deposited and linked to you, deposited but not linked to your IdHAL, or missing.
  • Lets you review paper by paper — type, disciplines, affiliation at the time of the work — with what is missing in red and what to double-check in amber.
  • Submits to HAL under your own account, after a "Check with HAL" that creates nothing. HAL moderators still review every record.

Your data stays with you

Runs on your computer

There is no bib2hal server. Your files are kept in a folder on your computer (Documents/bib2hal). Nothing is collected by the authors of the tool.

Your HAL password is never written to a file

It is checked with HAL when you sign in, then kept in memory or — if you choose — in your operating system's keychain.

Only public services, only what is needed

bib2hal only talks to Crossref and HAL, and only sends titles and DOIs to look references up. No tracking, no analytics.
