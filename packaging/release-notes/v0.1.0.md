# bib2hal 0.1.0 — first public release

bib2hal puts your publication list into HAL in a few clicks. Start from a BibTeX file (exported from
Google Scholar, Zotero, Mendeley or EndNote): bib2hal completes each reference, finds the ones already
in HAL, lets you review everything, and deposits the missing ones under your own HAL account.

Made by the AI4Health team @ DAISY (LIG, Université Grenoble Alpes).

## What it does

- **Import** a `.bib` file. Each reference is completed from Crossref (DOI, journal, pages, abstract)
  and looked up in HAL. Nothing is sent to HAL at this step.
- **Sort** every reference: *deposit* (not in HAL), *attach* (in HAL but not linked to your IdHAL),
  *skip* (already yours), *duplicate*, or *check* (possible homonym, unknown type, weak match).
- **Review** in a local web interface:
  - only the fields HAL needs for each document type (a thesis asks for the supervisor(s), a
    conference paper for the city, country and date…);
  - click the lab(s) you belonged to for each paper, with lab names shown;
  - discipline suggestions from your own shortlist;
  - attach a PDF you are allowed to share;
  - edit several references at once.
- **Check with HAL** before submitting: HAL validates each record completely and creates nothing.
- **Submit** to your HAL account. A reference is never sent twice. If HAL refuses a record, bib2hal
  explains why in plain words and shows only those records to fix.
- **Come back later**: your review is saved as you go. Re-import an updated `.bib` and only new
  references are analysed. *Export as .bib* gives you back your list with your corrections.

## Your data stays with you

- bib2hal runs only on your computer. There is no bib2hal server, no account and no tracking.
- It talks only to HAL and Crossref, and only to do what you ask.
- Your HAL password is never written to a file. It is kept in memory, or in your system keychain if
  you tick *Remember me*. *Sign out* removes it.
- Requests to HAL are spaced out, and bib2hal stops at once if HAL's anti-robot protection answers.

## Download

| System | File |
|---|---|
| Windows 10/11 | `bib2hal-windows-x64.exe` |
| macOS (Apple Silicon, M1 and later) | `bib2hal-macos-arm64.zip` |
| Linux (x64) | `bib2hal-linux-x64.zip` |

Double-click to start: the interface opens in your browser and your files are kept in
`Documents/bib2hal`. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lets you check the files you downloaded.

The applications are not signed yet, so your system warns you the first time: on **Windows** click
*More info → Run anyway*; on **macOS** right-click → *Open* → *Open*.

Guide: https://ai4health-daisy-lig.github.io/bib2hal/how-to.html

## Known limitations

- macOS builds are for Apple Silicon only (Intel Macs: install from source, see the README).
- HAL moderators review every deposit before it becomes public. bib2hal does not speed that up.
- *attach* records must still be linked to your IdHAL by hand in HAL (*Mon espace → Mes publications*).

Feedback and bug reports are welcome in the repository's Issues.
